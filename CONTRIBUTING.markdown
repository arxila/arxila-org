Contributing to Arxila: Terms and Conditions
============================================

Do you want to contribute your work to Arxila? Well, then first and most important: **THANK YOU!**

In order to accept your contribution, there are some terms you must expressly agree with, so please read them
carefully. They might look a bit cumbersome, but they are here in order to protect you, your contribution, and
especially the projects' future.

For how to contribute (issues, pull requests, code conventions), see the
[Arxila contribution guidelines](https://github.com/arxila/.github/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md).


Who can contribute?
-------------------

Anyone, with the only condition that they must be a **private individual**, acting in their own name, and not being
endorsed in their contributed work by any company or government.

This condition does not only refer to the ownership of the effort invested in contributing to the project, but also
to the fact that *no private or public company will be mentioned as a part of your contribution in the project's
website or code*, including but not limited to web or email addresses, or package names.


How do you agree to these terms?
--------------------------------

Submitting any contribution to an Arxila project implies your **full acceptance of these terms**, including the
*Arxila Individual Contributor License Agreement* below.

In addition, **every pull request must state explicitly that you have read and agree with these terms**. The pull
request template of Arxila projects includes this statement for you to tick; otherwise, write it yourself, for
example:

> I have read and agree with the Arxila contribution terms and the Arxila Individual Contributor License Agreement
> (version 20261126).

Pull requests that do not include such a statement will not be considered until they do.


If your employer could claim rights over your contribution
----------------------------------------------------------

All Arxila software is distributed under the **Apache License 2.0** open source license, and your contributions will
be licensed in the same way.

If you work for a company which, by the way or place in which your contribution was created, by your contract terms
or by the laws of your country, could claim any rights (including but not limited to intellectual or industrial
property) over your contribution, then before contributing, your company must authorize your contribution: someone
entitled to do so on its behalf must send the project maintainers a written statement that the company agrees with
these terms, that it authorizes your contribution to Arxila, and that it will never claim any kind of rights over it.
Open an issue in the project to ask the maintainers how to send it.
